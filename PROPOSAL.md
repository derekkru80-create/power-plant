---

## Executive Summary

This proposal outlines a 10 MW Powermax gasification power plant pilot project with integrated biochar production in High Level, Northern Alberta. This dual-purpose facility addresses critical regional and provincial challenges including emissions reduction, wildfire management, renewable energy baseload capacity, sustainable biomass utilization, and soil health improvement.

The facility will convert post-fire timber salvage and logging residues into:
1. **Clean, renewable baseload power** (10 MW continuous)
2. **High-value biochar** (5,000-25,000 tonnes/year) for agricultural soil improvement
3. **Carbon credits** through permanent carbon sequestration

This represents a transformational sustainable solution to Alberta's energy security, environmental commitments, and agricultural productivity challenges for 2050.

## Biochar Market & Farmer Economics

### The Biochar Advantage: Not a Fertilizer, But a Soil Investment

**Biochar vs. Fertilizer – Key Differences:**

| Feature | Fertilizer | Biochar |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **What It Does** | Feeds crop now | Improves soil permanently |
| **Duration** | 1 season | 10-20+ years (permanent) |
| **Re-application** | Every year | Once |
| **Nutrient Leaching** | 40-60% lost to leaching | No leaching—nutrient retention |
| **Drought Protection** | None | Holds 2-3x more water |
| **Microbial Life** | Vulnerable to disruption | Protects in soil pores |
| **Cost per Application** | Annual expense | One-time investment |

### 10-Year Farmer Economics (Per Hectare Wheat)

**Scenario 1: Conventional Agriculture (Baseline)**
- Annual urea (N fertilizer): 120 kg N/ha
- Annual fertilizer cost: $200-300/ha ($494-741 per acre)
- 10-year fertilizer cost: $2,000-3,000/ha
- Average yield: 4.4 tonnes/ha
- 10-year yield total: 44 tonnes/ha

**Scenario 2: Biochar Application (One-time)**
- Biochar application: 3.5 tonnes/ha (one-time)
- Biochar applied cost: $1,400/ha ($565/acre, one application)
- Reduced annual urea: 80 kg N/ha (33% reduction)
- Reduced annual fertilizer cost: $133-200/ha
- 10-year fertilizer cost: $1,330-2,000/ha
- **Yield increase:** 5% on poor soils → 4.62 tonnes/ha
- 10-year yield total: 46.2 tonnes/ha

**10-Year Total Cost Comparison (Per Hectare):**

| Item | Conventional | Biochar | Difference |
|------|-------------|---------|------------|
| Fertilizer (10 yrs) | $2,000-3,000 | $1,330-2,000 | Save $670-1,000 |
| Biochar (one-time) | $0 | $1,400 | +$1,400 |
| **Net 10-Year Cost** | $2,000-3,000 | $2,730-3,400 | **+$730-1,400** |
| **Yield Gain (tonnes)** | 44 | 46.2 (+5%) | +2.2 tonnes |
| **Value of Yield Gain** | — | — | **+$880-1,540** (@ $400-700/t) |
| **Farmer Position** | Baseline | Break-even to +$140 profit | ✅ **Profitable** |

**On Poor/Sandy/Acidic Soils (10% Yield Increase):**
- Yield increase: 4.4 → 4.84 tonnes/ha (+10%)
- 10-year yield gain: 4.4 tonnes
- Value of gain: $1,760-3,080/ha (@ $400-700/tonne wheat)
- **Farmer Position:** $360-1,680 profit per hectare over 10 years ✅

### Water Retention & Drought Protection
- **Biochar Water Holding Capacity:** Increases soil water retention by 20-30%
- **Benefit in Drought Years:** Protects yield in dry conditions (no data loss years)
- **Value:** Worth $300-500/ha in drought-prone regions over 10 years

### Chemical Compatibility & Soil Biology
- **Roundup Tolerance:** Biochar is permanent carbon; glyphosate does not degrade it
- **Microbial Protection:** Beneficial microbes hide in biochar pores, survive glyphosate exposure
- **Recovery Time:** 
  - Soil without biochar: Microbes recover in months
  - Soil with biochar: Microbes recover in weeks
- **Fertilizer Efficiency:** Biochar increases nutrient availability, can reduce N needs by 30%

**Investor Takeaway:** Biochar is compatible with conventional farming—it enhances not replaces it. Works with all standard sprays and farming practices.
